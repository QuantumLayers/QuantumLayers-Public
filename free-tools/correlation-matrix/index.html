How to read it

  • r runs from −1 (move in opposite directions) to +1 (move together). Around ±0.1 is negligible, ±0.3 moderate, ±0.5 and beyond strong — but what counts as “strong” depends on the field.
  • Pearson measures straight-line relationships and is sensitive to outliers. Spearman works on ranks, so it catches any consistently rising or falling relationship and shrugs off extreme values. When the two disagree a lot, look at the scatter plot.
  • p-values test “is r different from 0?”. With many columns you run many tests, so some will look significant by chance — the Holm correction (on by default) adjusts for that. A • marks pairs that stay significant at 5%.
  • Missing values are handled pairwise: each pair uses the rows where both columns have a number, and n is shown for every pair. ID-like columns (row numbers, customer_id, postcodes) are left out, because correlating labels is meaningless.
  • Correlation is not causation: two columns can move together because a third thing drives both.
