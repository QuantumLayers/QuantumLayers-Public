Statistical significance calculator

Is the difference between two groups real, or could it be chance? Compare two rates (sign-ups, clicks, defects) or two averages (order value, time on task) and get the p-value, a confidence interval and a plain-language verdict.

Free, no sign-up. All maths runs in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

How statistical significance works

  • Significant means a difference this large would rarely appear if the two groups were really the same — at 95% confidence, “rarely” is less than 5% of the time (p < 0.05). It says nothing about whether the difference is big enough to matter; check the confidence interval and effect size for that.
  • Rates or averages? Use the Conversion rates tab when each person either did or didn’t do something. Use Averages for amounts — revenue, minutes, scores.
  • Skewed data (a few huge orders) makes averages unreliable. Paste the raw values in the Averages tab to also get a Mann–Whitney test, which compares the groups by rank instead.
  • Many comparisons inflate false positives: test 20 metrics and one will look significant by luck. Decide the main metric up front.
  • Methods. Conversion rates: two-sided pooled two-proportion z-test (equivalent to a 2×2 chi-square), Wald interval for the difference, delta-method interval for the relative lift. Averages: Welch’s t-test and Cohen’s d; Mann–Whitney U on raw values. Sample size: normal approximation for two proportions. All checked against SciPy.
