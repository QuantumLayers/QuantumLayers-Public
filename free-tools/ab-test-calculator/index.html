What the numbers mean

  • p-value — if A and B were truly identical, how often would a gap at least this big show up by chance? Below 0.05 is the usual bar for “significant” at 95% confidence.
  • Confidence interval — the range of lifts that fit your data. If it spans zero, the data can’t rule out “no effect” — even if the point estimate looks great.
  • Sample ratio mismatch — if you meant to split traffic 50:50 but got 52:48 on a big sample, something in the assignment or tracking is broken, and the result can’t be trusted whatever the p-value says.
  • Don’t peek and stop. Checking every day and stopping the first time p dips under 0.05 inflates false positives a lot. Decide the sample size up front (planner tab) and evaluate once.
  • Methods. Conversion rates: two-sided pooled two-proportion z-test (equivalent to a 2×2 chi-square), Wald interval for the difference, delta-method interval for the relative lift. Averages: Welch’s t-test (no equal-variance assumption) and Cohen’s d; Mann–Whitney U on raw values. Sample size: normal approximation for two proportions. All checked against SciPy.
